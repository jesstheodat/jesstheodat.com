About

I’ve spent nearly two decades working on the systems behind the systems.

I’ve worn a lot of hats along the way—web developer, IT consultant, systems administrator, network engineer, reliability engineer, and security technical lead across startups, universities, and Big Tech. That range gave me a practical understanding of how applications, networks, infrastructure, operations, and security fit together—and where they tend to fail each other.

At Google, I became the company’s first Black woman Site Reliability Engineer. Over time, my work shifted from keeping large systems reliable to securing the infrastructure and trust decisions underneath them.

As a Senior Site Reliability Engineer and Security Tech Lead, I’ve worked across several security domains. I’ve led efforts to protect enterprise compute and storage infrastructure from insider threats and other risks; designed zero-trust architectures, vulnerability-management programs, and security capability roadmaps; and helped raise security maturity across a mix of on-prem and cloud environments.

Today, I work on Core Security Infrastructure spanning identity, access, and software supply chain security—in plain English, the systems that help decide who gets access to what and which software can be trusted to run.

A big part of my job is taking ambiguous security problems, breaking them down, and getting them to a point where teams can make good decisions and move forward. That often means making the tradeoffs clear, bringing people with different priorities into alignment, and helping shape risk and architecture decisions that work for the organization as a whole.

I’ve also been self-hosting, automating, and building on-premise systems for years. Privacy has long been part of that practice. I’m now expanding that work professionally, with a focus on helping executives and other high-risk individuals understand how scattered pieces of digital exposure can become meaningful risk once they’re connected.

Career journey

Built layer by layer

Nearly two decades moving deeper into the systems behind everyday technology. Each chapter added another layer to how I build, operate, secure, and reason about them.

Privacy risk often comes less from any single data point and more from how different pieces of information connect to reveal a larger picture. A location can become a movement pattern. And a handful of unrelated details can begin to describe behavior.

I’m now applying the same systems and risk lens professionally to digital exposure—helping executives and other high-risk individuals understand how disconnected pieces of information become actionable when they are combined, and reduce the information and access paths that create meaningful risk.
