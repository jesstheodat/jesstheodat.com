Systems mindset

About

I build the kinds of systems most people never see but rely on every day.

My work focuses on core security infrastructure across identity and access, software supply chain security, and the systems that support secure, reliable infrastructure at scale.

My background in site reliability engineering shapes how I approach security. I assume systems will eventually be stressed, misused, misunderstood, or asked to operate outside the conditions their designers originally imagined. The useful question is rarely whether pressure arrives; it is whether the foundations were designed to hold through pressure, ambiguity, and growth.

Over time, my focus has moved beyond tools toward judgment. The work that matters most to me is helping teams make sound decisions early—reasoning clearly about tradeoffs, dependencies, incentives, failure modes, and second-order effects while course correction is still relatively inexpensive.

The same systems mindset follows me outside of work. Self-hosted infrastructure and automation give me a smaller laboratory for thinking about resilience, dependency reduction, local control, and privacy.

I’m also exploring how that perspective applies to defensive digital privacy: understanding digital exposure, reducing unnecessary risk, and helping people make more deliberate choices about what information they leave behind.

What has always mattered to me is building what ages well: technical and organizational systems meant to outlast any single team, roadmap, or moment—the kind that earn trust, reduce fragility, and keep doing their job long after the spotlight moves on.

Areas of focus

  • Security Architecture Identity and access, software supply chain security, and foundational infrastructure security.
  • Site Reliability Engineering Reliable systems, operational resilience, dependencies, and failure-aware design.
  • Technical Leadership Architecture decisions, systems thinking, risk, tradeoffs, and long-horizon technical judgment.
  • Self-Hosting & Automation Local systems, infrastructure ownership, automation, and resilient personal technology.
  • Defensive Digital Privacy Digital exposure awareness, privacy hardening, and reducing unnecessary information risk.
